DSCR loans explained

Qualify for a 30-year rental loan on the property's rent instead of your tax returns. Here's the math, the requirements and the traps.

By Private.Money Editorial TeamUpdated October 30, 202610 min read

Key takeaways

  • DSCR = monthly rent ÷ PITIA (principal, interest, taxes, insurance, association dues).
  • 1.00x is break-even; most lenders want 1.00–1.25x or higher, and some go below 1.00x at lower leverage.
  • Typical 2026 terms: 30-year loans, 75–85% LTV purchases, starting rates roughly 5.75–7% for strong files.
  • Watch the prepayment penalty. It can cost thousands if you sell or refinance early.

What is a DSCR loan?

A debt service coverage ratio (DSCR) loan is a long-term, business-purpose mortgage for rental property that is underwritten primarily on the property's income. Instead of verifying your W-2s or tax returns, the lender asks one main question: does the rent cover the payment? That makes DSCR loans popular with self-employed investors, people scaling beyond conventional loan limits, and BRRRR investors refinancing out of short-term debt.

The DSCR formula

For residential rental loans, lenders typically use:

DSCR = Gross monthly rent ÷ PITIA
PITIA = principal + interest + property taxes + insurance + HOA/association dues (monthly)

Commercial lenders often use net operating income divided by annual debt service instead, which subtracts operating expenses first. Always ask which method a lender uses.

A worked example

Monthly market rent$2,600
Loan amount$240,000 at 7.25%, 30-year amortizing
Principal & interest≈ $1,637
Taxes ($3,600/yr) + insurance ($1,500/yr)$425 / month
PITIA≈ $2,062
DSCR$2,600 ÷ $2,062 = ≈ 1.26x

At 1.26x, this property would qualify with most DSCR lenders and likely earn better pricing than a file near 1.00x. Try your own numbers in the DSCR calculator, which also shows the maximum loan the rent supports at 1.00x and 1.25x.

What moves your rate

  • Leverage: each step down in LTV usually improves pricing.
  • DSCR: 1.25x+ often prices better than 1.00–1.24x; sub-1.00x costs more.
  • Credit score: pricing tiers commonly break at 680, 700, 720 and 740.
  • Prepayment penalty: accepting a longer penalty usually lowers your rate.
  • Loan size and property type: very small loans, condos and short-term rentals may carry add-ons.

Prepayment penalties, decoded

Most DSCR loans include a prepayment penalty because lenders sell or securitize them expecting several years of interest. Common structures:

  • 5-4-3-2-1: 5% of the balance if repaid in year 1, 4% in year 2, and so on down to 0% after year 5.
  • 3-2-1: a three-year step-down.
  • Fixed percentage: for example, 3% for three years.
  • No prepay: available, usually at a noticeably higher rate.

On a $240,000 loan, a 5% first-year penalty is $12,000. If you might sell within a few years, pay for a shorter penalty.

Pros and cons

Pros
  • No personal income verification
  • Close in an LLC
  • No limit on number of properties (lender-specific)
  • 30-year fixed options
Cons
  • Higher rates than conventional loans
  • Prepayment penalties
  • Larger down payments
  • Rent appraisals can come in low

Frequently asked questions

Can I use short-term rental income?

Many programs accept projected or historical short-term rental income using market data tools or 12-month history, often with stricter leverage.

Can a DSCR loan pay off my hard money loan?

Yes. That's the "refinance" in BRRRR. Ask about seasoning: some lenders use the new appraised value immediately, while others require months of ownership. See BRRRR financing.
